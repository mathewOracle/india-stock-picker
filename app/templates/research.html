Research & validation

Evidence layer: nothing is called a Buy unless the backtest, news and (if proven useful) ML model back it.

{{ refresh_button('research', 'Run research (backtest, news, model)') }}

Incremental: the 10-year backtest ledger and the news archive only add new dates (a few seconds to minutes after the first run). Previous results stay in place if a step fails.

How we avoid fooling ourselves

  • No lookahead: every past decision uses only data up to that day; trades enter at the next day's open; if stop and target are both hit in a day we assume the stop. A test proves cutting off the future changes nothing.
  • Control group: the rules must beat the average of all evaluations (not just zero), so a rising market doesn't count as skill.
  • Costs: 0.15% per side (brokerage, STT, slippage) is charged on every trade.
  • Pass rules fixed up front (30+ trades, t-stat of 1.65 or more) and never adjusted after seeing results. The recent-years window below is shown for context only: choosing a window after the fact would be data snooping.
  • Permanent, append-only history: the ledger keeps every past evaluation; only new dates and still-open trades are computed. If Yahoo restates a stock's prices, only that stock is replayed.
  • Fundamentals are not backtested: Yahoo only keeps 4-5 years of statements, so replaying today's numbers into 2018 would be cheating.
  • Survivorship: the universe is today's large caps, which flatters every result a little. The control group has the same bias, so the edge comparison stays fair.

How to read this honestly

  • A rule that "works" in a backtest can still fail live, and an edge that fades on newer data (as seen on the hold-out) is the usual way it fails. Passing is necessary, not sufficient.
  • "Not validated" does not mean the stock will fall. It means we have no evidence these rules pick winners better than chance.
  • The backtest tests the price half of the rules. The fundamental half is judged on standard practice only.
